# Voiceover & Narration Scripts

> Timecoded scripts for both shorts. Narration is sparse by design — the visuals lead, the voice seals emotion. Delivery notes included for ElevenLabs / actor direction.

---

## PROJECT 1 — "Mochi and the Moon Jar"

**Format:** Warm narrator (female, mid-30s, soft British or gentle American), storybook tone.
**ElevenLabs voice suggestion:** Charlotte, Matilda, or a custom cloned "bedtime story" voice.
**Total VO:** ~45 words across 90 seconds. Let the music and visuals breathe.

### Script

```
[0:00–0:08]  (silence — only ambient wind + a soft music sting)
                    NARRATOR (whispered, almost wondering)
              "They say the moon is too big to hold..."

[0:08–0:22]  (silence — sfx: meteor hiss, magical chime)

[0:22–0:30]         NARRATOR (gentle, smiling)
              "But Mochi had never been very good at listening."

[0:30–0:45]  (silence — laughter sfx, music swells playful)

[0:45–0:58]         NARRATOR (soft, concerned)
              "Some things were never meant to be kept.
               Even the most precious ones."

[0:58–1:18]  (silence — wind, glass-cracking sfx, orchestral build)

[1:18–1:26]         NARRATOR (tender, triumphant)
              "And some friends... you love enough to let go."

[1:26–1:30]  (music resolves, final chime, fade to black)
                    NARRATOR (whispered end-cap)
              "Goodnight, little moon."
```

### Delivery Notes
- Pause for **at least 1.5s** after every line. The silence is the performance.
- First line: breathy, 15% slower than natural. Treat it like a secret.
- "Let go" line: slight tremor, but do NOT cry. Restraint sells it.
- End-cap "Goodnight, little moon" — whisper close to the mic, ASMR-adjacent.

## PROJECT 2 — "Signal from Kepler-9"

**Format:** Two voices.
- **ARIA (in-world):** tired, controlled, 30s female. Ridley-Scott-heroine energy.
- **ARIA-ECHO (the transmission):** same voice, but pitched -2 semitones, reverb tail, subtle digital crackle.
**ElevenLabs voice suggestion:** Rachel or Sarah with "stability 60%, similarity 85%, style 20%."
**Total VO:** ~90 words across 120 seconds.

### Script

```
[0:00–0:04]  (black screen, static)
                    ARIA-ECHO (distant, fractured)
              "...is anyone... listening?"

[0:04–0:10]  (hard cut to her eye)
                    ARIA (breath held, whisper)
              "Log entry. Day four hundred and twelve.
               The relay's dying. And I'm alone."

[0:10–0:25]  (station corridor wide — no VO, just hum and dripping coolant)

[0:25–0:35]         ARIA (alert, professional)
              "Incoming transmission. Source: Kepler-9b.
               Forty light-years out. That's... impossible."

[0:35–0:45]         ARIA-ECHO (through the hologram)
              "This is Dr. Aria Venn. If you're hearing this,
               you need to stop listening. Now."

[0:45–1:00]         ARIA (to herself, unraveling)
              "That's my voice.
               That is my voice."

[1:00–1:10]  (montage — diagnostics, no VO, just rising synths and glitch stabs)

[1:10–1:20]         ARIA (quiet, trembling)
              "System check complete. Transmission is clean.
               There's no error. There's no error."

[1:20–1:35]  (she opens the sealed compartment)
                    ARIA (barely audible)
              "Oh god."

[1:35–1:50]         ARIA-ECHO (soft, almost kind)
              "You were never the first, Aria.
               You were never the only one."

[1:50–2:00]  (cosmic pull-back, eye-nebula reveal)
                    ARIA-ECHO (final line, faint, smiling)
              "Welcome home."

[2:00]       (cut to black)
```

### Delivery Notes
- **ARIA's "That is my voice."** — first time neutral, second time cracks on "is." Do not repeat the line identically; the duplication is the horror.
- **ARIA-ECHO** should always feel 10% warmer than ARIA. The duplicate isn't evil — it's inevitable. That contrast is the twist.
- "Oh god" — do not over-act. One exhale. One syllable cracked.
- "Welcome home" — smile through the line. Let the listener feel the smile more than hear it.

## Subtitle / Caption Style Guide

- Cartoon: hand-drawn serif font, cream color, 2% drop shadow, centered-bottom, soft fade.
- Sci-fi: monospaced terminal font (JetBrains Mono), white on 40% black bar, typewriter reveal at 30 chars/sec, lower-third.
- Both: burn-in captions for the first 30s (hook window) — watch-time boost on muted autoplay.
